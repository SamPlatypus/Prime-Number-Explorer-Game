How the sieve works

An idea about 2,200 years old

A way to find every prime number up to a limit, credited to Eratosthenes of Cyrene, the Greek scholar who also measured the size of the Earth.

  1. Write out the numbers from 2 up to your limit. 1 sits out: it's neither prime nor composite.
  2. Take the first number that isn't crossed out. It's prime, so it lights up in its own colour.
  3. Cross out every multiple of it. You can start at its square, because the smaller multiples were already caught by smaller primes.
  4. Move on to the next number still standing, and repeat.
  5. Once a prime's square is bigger than your limit, stop. Everything still standing is prime.
  • Tap any number, any time, to see its factors.
  • Each crossed-out number takes the colour of its smallest prime factor, the prime that caught it.
  • Try 6 columns: after 2 and 3, every prime lands in just two columns, one either side of a multiple of 6.
